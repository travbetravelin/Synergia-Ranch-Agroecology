Biochar is a carbon-rich material produced by burning organic matter in low-oxygen conditions (a process called pyrolysis). Applied to soil, it improves water retention, supports microbial life, sequesters carbon, and rebuilds degraded [[soil-health]] over time.

At the [[2026-water-conference]], [[james-skeet]] of [[spirit-farm]] presented on the use of biochar in the semi-arid Southwest, drawing on both Navajo traditional knowledge and contemporary research. Biochar works synergistically with [[composting]] to build lasting soil fertility and improve [[water-management]] at the farm scale.

Biochar is a key tool in [[regenerative-farming]] and plays a role in addressing [[desertification]] by restoring the water-holding capacity of depleted soils. It can be produced on-site from agricultural waste, making it accessible to small-scale land stewards.
