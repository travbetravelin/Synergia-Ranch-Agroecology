Desertification is the process by which fertile, productive land gradually becomes desert — stripped of vegetation, topsoil, and the capacity to hold water. It is driven by overgrazing, poor land management, deforestation, and climate change, and it represents one of the most urgent ecological challenges facing the Southwest and much of the world.

The [[2026-water-conference]] was convened specifically to address regenerative solutions to desertification, bringing together practitioners from across disciplines and traditions. Speakers including [[mark-nelson]], [[indra-shekhar-singh]], [[james-skeet]], and [[jan-willem-jansens]] each approached the problem from different angles — ecological engineering, [[indigenous-knowledge]], community science, and landscape planning.

Reversing desertification requires restoring [[water-management]] cycles, rebuilding [[soil-health]], replanting native vegetation, and reintegrating [[agroforestry]] and [[regenerative-farming]] practices. [[erosion-control]] is often the first step — slowing water long enough for it to sink in rather than run off.
