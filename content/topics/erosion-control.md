title: Erosion Control

Erosion control encompasses the techniques and structures used to slow the movement of water and wind across bare or degraded land, preventing the loss of topsoil and allowing water to infiltrate rather than run off. It is often the first practical step in reversing [[desertification]].

Methods include check dams, brush barriers, grass hedges, contour earthworks, rock spreaders, and the strategic planting of native vegetation. Many of these techniques draw on [[indigenous-knowledge]] — traditional water harvesting and land management systems that have kept landscapes productive for centuries.

At the [[2026-water-conference]], [[jan-willem-jansens]] and [[amanda-bramble]] both addressed erosion control as part of broader [[water-management]] and [[soil-health]] restoration work across [[new-mexico]]. [[mike-halverson]]'s native plant work at Santa Ana also contributes directly to erosion stabilisation.
