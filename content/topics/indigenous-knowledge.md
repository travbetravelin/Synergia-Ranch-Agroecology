Indigenous knowledge refers to the traditional, ancestral, and place-based knowledge systems developed by communities over generations of close relationship with specific landscapes. These systems offer proven, culturally grounded approaches to land, water, food, and ecological stewardship.

At the [[2026-water-conference]], indigenous knowledge was a recurring thread. [[indra-shekhar-singh]] presented on decentralised water systems rooted in South Asian tradition. [[james-skeet]] and [[joyce-skeet]] of [[spirit-farm]] drew on Navajo land wisdom in their work with [[biochar]] and [[botanical-knowledge]]. [[mike-halverson]] brought the Pueblo of Santa Ana's plant knowledge to discussions of restoration.

Indigenous knowledge is increasingly recognised as essential alongside Western science in addressing [[desertification]], [[water-management]], and [[soil-health]] — not as a supplement but as a primary framework for sustainable land stewardship.
